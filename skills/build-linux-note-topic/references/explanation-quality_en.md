# Explain writing methods and readers’ understanding and acceptance

[简体中文](explanation-quality.md) | **English**

This reference is used to newly write, supplement or rewrite the technical teaching text, especially to deal with the feedback of "obscure", "like an outline" and "only teaches copy commands". Pure typesetting tasks still comply with the existing content protection scope.

Reading order: first use Section 8 to determine the rhythm and prerequisite knowledge of the textbook, and then use Sections 1-7 to check whether the explanations and experiments are valid; Sections 9-11 are used for exercise design, tone calibration and sample chapter acceptance. The check items here are author tools, not title templates that require the text to be applied item by item.

## 1. The conditions for completion are understanding and migration

The facts are correct, the terms are defined, the commands can be executed, and the readers understand them. These are four different requirements. Passing the first three items does not automatically prove that the fourth item is passed. The text should allow readers with a user-specified foundation to explain the problem, predict the results, complete the operation, and know what to change and what not to change when switching to their own project.

When users emphasize "explain clearly at all costs", priority is given to ensuring the completeness of the explanation within the scope of the authorized topic. Do not deliver outline-style teaching materials in order to shorten the response, save tokens, or end the round as quickly as possible; continue to complete it if you need to continue collecting evidence, rewriting, opening chapters, or checking. This request does not extend the subject matter, operational authority, or external publication authorization, nor does it require unlimited addition of background irrelevant to current understanding.

Zero base is based on the knowledge required for the current task as the boundary. For C/C++ developers in this repository, common mechanism models, interface examples, and supporting tests are first implemented in C/C++. The burden of prerequisites for languages ​​such as Python cannot be increased in order to write less code. If the task itself is to use a tool or library that relies on Python, explain why this dependency is necessary; being familiar with C functions does not mean that the reader already knows the relationship between Python module search, project entry, and package installation. At this time, the necessary writing methods and operation organization will be explained with the help of existing programming experience, and irrelevant language courses will not be carried out. The Python example here is used to illustrate the depth of explanation of necessary exceptions. It is not authorized to arbitrarily replace ordinary C/C++ teaching with Python.

## 2. Introduce the name from the task without asking the reader to memorize the noun first.

Let the reader first see the specific problem and why the existing methods are insufficient, and then introduce the objects needed to solve this gap. Explain the object's role in the task at hand, giving the necessary precise definitions, and then let the reader observe how it works.

If a sentence must use four or five yet-to-be-established names simultaneously, the learning phase usually needs to be broken up. Don't keep the original jumping text by adding a big glossary. Terminology entry cards, classification matrices and concept coverage tables mainly constrain whether the author misses information and should not mechanically become the fixed opening of each section.

Likewise, definitions cannot simply be replaced with life metaphors. After the metaphor establishes intuition, it should also fall back to the real object, data, scope and boundaries. Comparison tables are suitable for organizing differences that are already understood, but are not suitable for taking on the responsibility of explaining all concepts for the first time.

## 3. Expand the conclusion to the point where it can be deduced

"Activating the environment only modifies PATH" is a conclusion, not a complete explanation. Readers also need to know: who is looking for the executable file when entering the short command python; what is the search order; why another Python is not automatically selected when switching the working directory; which directory is placed where by the activation script; why this selection can be bypassed when directly writing the path of another interpreter. Then compare the results with the actual interpreter path to provide evidence for the conclusion.

"west init initialize, west update update" just repeats the command name. A list that declares two repositories should be used as an example to explain which files are there before and after init, which repositories have not been retrieved, what declarations are read by update, which repositories are downloaded, and which commits are selected. Readers should be able to predict why the working directory has not changed when "only the list is changed but not updated".

"Recommended fixed version" and "this is generally the case in the industry" also need basis. Explain what kind of collaboration or replication problem it solves, what it costs to maintain, and under what circumstances an alternative is needed. The behaviors allowed by the tool, team conventions, and simplifications made by the author for teaching should be stated separately.

These examples are a reference for in-depth explanation, and do not require all topics to copy Python or west content.

## 4. Make commands and configuration part of the reasoning

Naturally explain the following information near the operation, and organize it into a coherent text according to the task needs. There is no need to mechanically repeat the six subheadings:

1. What problems are you currently encountering and why do you need to perform this step now?
2. From where it is executed, which program handles it, and who the relative path is based on.
3. What do the actual parameters and inputs used mean, and why was this value chosen?
4. What files, process states, or configurations were read, created, or modified; what is the scope.
5. What results should be observed, what relationships should be satisfied between the results, and what the exit code or output can and cannot prove.
6. How to narrow the scope and restore the scene based on evidence when a failure occurs; how to predict the result after changing a condition.

Split command blocks by causal state changes. Don't put a screen of commands up front and all the explanations far away. Don't just say "modify the file yourself" either: when you first ask for a change, identify the file, the context, the content of the change, the program that reads it, and when it will take effect.

For example, the interpretation of --no-build-isolation cannot stop at "turn off build isolation". It should also be noted that the default build will prepare a separate build dependency environment; when turned off, it will use the existing build backend in the current environment, so the backend must be installed first, this parameter itself will not install it for you.

The output focuses on writing verifiable relationships, and does not treat local absolute paths, random commit numbers, or complete logs as constants that readers must match. Actual return values ​​must be distinguished from hypothetical representations.

### 4.1. Pairing of commands and instructions at the reading position

Relevant explanations first provide conspicuous anchor points, allowing readers to directly know which object and location the current text explains, and then understand the mechanism. Anchors must reuse the original names and identifiers in the example: block line numbers and original symbols for code; file names and sections/keys for configuration; actual lines or fields for output; original node names, arrow actions, or step numbers for diagrams. When multiple examples are adjacent to each other, indicate the example referred to first. Do not just write "here, it, the above parameters, this process" to let readers speculate. Anchors appear close to the explanation, not concentrated on distant indexes, or stacked with multiple sets of numbers per sentence. The text needs to support rapid positioning, and thinking should be spent on cause and effect and migration, rather than guessing where the author is pointing.

When users use a single paragraph to point out positioning problems common to a topic, use this paragraph as a checking example to check similar explanations chapter by chapter according to the scope of authorization. At the same time, check navigation, command attachments, and section numbers left after rearrangement. The maintenance record lists the inspection objects and processing results of each chapter; paragraphs that meet the requirements can be retained, and sample repairs or skill repairs cannot be used to replace the implementation of the entire set of text.

**First find the object, and then talk about the line number inside the object.** When readers are required to open, read, modify or check files, databases, logs, configurations, or products for the first time, they must explain the actual name and nature, the project/instance to which it belongs, the method of determining the location, which step it was generated from, and whether it should currently exist before the operation. Relative paths must have a clear root directory; multiple environments or multiple build directories must explain the selection basis and give an example that can be directly located. When the tool output is in the terminal and has not been saved, it says to check the terminal output of which command and does not create a log file. Just giving "generated file", "build directory", "above database" or the correct file name is still not closed; nor is it mechanically cramming the entire machine's absolute path into every sentence.

When switching applications, tools, build directories, terminals, configuration branches, or working copies, first indicate which input and results will be read in the next section. Products that have not yet been produced can only be previewed and cannot be used as ready-made inspection objects. Chapter references should fall to specific subsections, and when performing an operation, they must point to specific commands or named attachments in the text; directory indexes are for navigation and cannot be written as runnable scripts. Existing in-place commands are text-based, preventing readers from traversing the index first to find the command.

**Path writing follows the actual reader.** Positioning, directory entry and file operations in the terminal use the path syntax of the terminal; the file manager address, program parameters, and configuration file contents indicate the usage environment respectively. When different runtimes on the same host need to convert paths, the conversion method should be given at the junction point and which program should read the results; one path form cannot be used to run through all layers, nor can existing tool parameters be globally replaced for a unified appearance. The main text description and the immediate command must use a consistent path reference, especially check whether the reader can use the path in the description directly for the indicated operation.

The operation steps for beginners are preferably "Current purpose → a command or an undetachable unit → next to explanation and result → next step". The command can be copied directly, and the explanation should point out the parameters, processing objects and changed status; you cannot just write "the first...the second..." after a long block to force the reader to search upwards repeatedly. Successive identical operations can be merged after the rules are established, but each branch, environment change, and new parameter must still be accounted for in place.

The first line of each executable block indicates the working directory with a comment for the target shell, and the interpreter/environment if necessary. If there are commands after cd within the block, update the location comment; "Execute in the above directory" is not enough to support readers to reconnect the steps. Comments are location hints and do not pretend to have been executed by cd. Code is only used for presentation, document content, or alternatives to avoid confusion with sequential execution routes.

Here-documents, subshells, variable scopes, and exit codes that need to be read immediately are semantically preserved as complete units and do not break runtime behavior for chunking. Add comments close to the commands within the unit, and explain the overall results after the block. Use a short list when options or file responsibilities are parallel items, and use tables for status comparisons; cause and effect explanations are still written in paragraphs, and not all content is mechanically tabulated.

When parameters are interpreted after a long command or configuration block, it must be possible to navigate directly from the interpretation to the actual location within the block. It is preferable to use the correspondence table of "line number within block + original parameter fragment + function", or indicate the specific line number and original fragment in the adjacent text; directly add the position column to the existing parameter table without repeating the explanation in another copy. Multiple code blocks each correspond to their own description, and alternative routes must not reference the line numbers of another block. Line numbers start from the first line in the fence, comments and blank lines are included, display breaks are not included; recalculate after code changes. Do not impose line-by-line tables in short commands when the parameters are already visible at a glance.

No naked line numbers are inserted in the reproducible code, and no comments are inserted between Bash backslashes or PowerShell backtick continuation lines. Stages can be marked with legal comments before the complete command, and the marks can be reused in the text; specific parameters that still need to be explained must be located in the corresponding lines. When multiple parameters are squeezed into one line and it is difficult to compare, wrap the parameters according to the responsibility while maintaining correct semantics and line continuation. The line-by-line correspondence must explain who gave the parameters, what was changed, or how to verify them. You cannot just copy the original commands into the table; the causal explanation across steps should still be retained in the coherent text. When delivering, check the anchor point from each association explanation: whether it uniquely points to the real location in the current example, whether it can be found without guessing, whether the reference line contains the pointed fragment, and whether the attachments are consistent. Then check actions such as "Open/View/Run/Check" one by one to confirm that a unique object can be selected based only on the current location and clear previous steps, without relying on chat, full search, or author memory. Recording the specific breakpoints found and the location of repairs; link existence, line number matching and script passing can only prove the formal check and cannot replace this positioning review.

### 4.2. Create the experimental project first, and then let the tool manage it

Before the first layout-dependent command, explain what role the directory represents, who created it, when it existed, which independent project/repository the source files belong to, and how they collaborate at runtime. Don't rely on names like remotes, app, src, build, etc. to convey meaning on their own. The directory tree distinguishes between the current state and the completed state, and directories that do not yet exist are explicitly marked to be generated.

When multiple knowledge layers are superimposed, specific mappings are given first, for example, "The language module is the import object, the Git repository saves the version, and the list matches the versions and locations of multiple repositories." Many folders do not automatically equal multiple repositories, and the version tool does not automatically establish the import or construction relationship of the language when obtaining the source code. Provide observable identification methods and use these differences to explain subsequent results.

The key configuration name that appears for the first time should distinguish between tool default, configurable convention and custom name in this example, and explain how to find the truly effective file when opening an unfamiliar project. Don't defer necessary positioning rules to a later chapter; and don't ask to see the artifacts of an action before you create it. Allows a preview of "X will be generated next" and then performs the creation before showing the actual location, minimal content, and field meaning. The success of an example should also be transferred to the method of understanding other people's projects.

## 5. The text and experiments are their own responsibilities.

In the continuous textbook, the main text is responsible for both explanation and hands-on experiments of this unit; the special materials directory stores the source code, configuration, input data and necessary preparation tools consistent with the main text. By default, another set of comprehensive operation manuals will not be separated, allowing readers to splice back and forth between the main text and practical training. The README description file of the material catalog is enough to describe the ownership, purpose and corresponding chapters, and does not form a second teaching route by itself.

The unit experiment should be focused: first list the knowledge that readers have mastered so far in this unit, and then check the dependencies of the code, commands, expected output and exercise solutions one by one. Only the necessary increments are introduced for the current problem; subsequent knowledge such as packaging, testing framework, and plug-in extensions cannot be mixed into primary units such as environment creation in advance due to the reuse of comprehensive training. Successive chapters can use the clearly stated product of the previous chapter, and clearly indicate the current position and required status; the entire prerequisite course cannot be repeated for the purpose of "independently closing any page". When the user clearly needs to independently experiment with the article, define its pre-edit boundary separately and do not force it to be connected to the primary main line.

The main text should show the complete applet, configuration and file location that need to be understood or modified this time; the first appearance should not only link to the source code, or use ellipses to cover up the content that determines whether the experiment can run. As the instance grows, you can show clear differences in the fully created file, but you need to give the modification location and context to avoid readers guessing the insertion point. For larger third-party source codes, you can select relevant units that are complete enough and explain the version and context. You cannot copy the entire project for "full-text embedding".

Obtain materials, core operations, observations, reasons, modifications and restorations and arrange them on the spot in the order of understanding. Links are used for material downloads, pre-chapter navigation, and in-depth reading that does not affect the main line; performing experiments should not require jumping back and forth across multiple pages. Comprehensive exercises at the end of the chapter can only combine previously learned knowledge and provide well-founded answers.

When receiving feedback that "the text is obscure", we will identify where readers will get stuck chapter by chapter, and revise the explanations and connections there. Increasing the overview, more links, scripts or the number of tests does not automatically count as a text fix.

The auxiliary script should explain what it prepares for the experiment and how it differs from the target tool's responsibilities. If the script encapsulates the core commands that should be learned, the core steps should be expanded; if it is only responsible for creating test materials, it can be retained, but the reader should know the input and generated results. Don’t let “one-click success” overshadow the learned mechanics.

## 6. Cold reading to check the actual understanding process

Read chapter by chapter in reader order, without resorting to unwritten premises in the author's mind, and answer:

- Why is this a problem? What might the reader have done originally, and what would have gone wrong?
- What real objects are being processed? Who holds the state, who reads the configuration, who triggers changes?
- Why did you choose this command, parameter, or code modification? What happens if you change the value?
- How does the expected phenomenon follow from the previous explanation? If the results are inconsistent, which piece of evidence should be looked at first?
- Where should I change when transplanting it to my own project? What dependencies or invariants need to be preserved?

Temporarily ignore the reference link and check whether the current chapter can still complete the promised explanation. Then search backwards from each command, output, recommendation and conclusion to find the premise it is based on; if you can't find it, add the text instead of just "see official documents". Deep details are allowed to be cited, and the indispensable bridge to current reasoning must be stated in situ.

Also read across headings one by one according to Section 8.1, especially checking definitions, tables, codes, and switches before and after experiments. A qualified background at the beginning of the chapter does not mean that the cohesion within the chapter is qualified; the title has summarized a certain conclusion, nor does it mean that the main text has explained it.

Automated checks can verify links, syntax, metadata, and experimental results, but cannot prove that the text is understandable. The author cannot claim to have passed the trial reading for real beginners by doing independent reading review on his own. Document verification methods and practical limitations.

## 7. Document concrete fixes, not formal completions

The design and review records should indicate the specific breakpoints and supplementary positions, for example, "The original text directly gives the activation command, now the short command search is explained first, and then another interpreter is directly called." Avoid recording only "readability has been enhanced" and "all terms have been covered".

The actual rewritten chapter scope, experimental verification scope, and remaining limitations will be stated upon delivery. If the text is too large, break it into chapters based on learning dependencies and maintain navigation. Do not reduce the scope of user-authorized content by deleting explanations. User-facing progress and delivery can be brief, with detailed explanations left in maintainable teaching materials.

### 7.1. Reconstruction of continuous text from questioning

Review comments are evidence to locate problems in the text, not a list of new paragraphs. When a reader points out that a certain sentence is not understood, that sentence is the breakpoint of understanding, not necessarily the position where a patch should be inserted. First, restore the understanding process based on clear feedback and the existing text to determine whether the gap comes from conceptual prerequisites, mechanism explanations, operating conditions, or chapter sequence; do not treat the limitations or wrong models speculated by the author as claims that have been expressed by readers, and then refute them. Check where the entire topic establishes these premises, and then determine the scope of continuous revision. If necessary, adjust the foreshadowing of the previous chapter, the current section, and the continuation of the following chapter. Chat replies can directly answer questions, and the teaching material must allow readers who have never seen chat to naturally get the answer along with the previous text.

When revising, keep the same scene, participants, and observation targets, put the missing premise before the inference that relies on it for the first time, and let the new explanation enter the original causal chain. The original paragraphs, chart introductions and endings should be adjusted accordingly; old supplements covered by new explanations should be merged or replaced, and the valid information in them should be retained. Multiple rounds of Q&A should not be left side by side. If a change of perspective is needed, it should be prompted by an unresolved issue in the current scene, rather than re-declaring a boundary in each paragraph or refuting a misunderstanding that the reader has not yet developed.

The text only retains the background, necessary conditions, and causal explanations of the learning object itself. The reason for the review, the user's wording, the revision commitment, the coverage list and the verification process are entered into the work record; they must not be written as "for the question just now", "the scene in the user's attachment" or item-by-item answers. User approval of supplementary content means that the content should be integrated into the teaching material and does not require the approval process to be explained in the text.

Hide the chat questions before delivery, read the end of the previous section, the revised section and the beginning of the next section, and then check five qualities along the topic route: readability requires natural sentences to bear explanations, fluency requires objects and causes and effects to be continuous across paragraphs, guidance requires readers to know why new content appears when it appears, professionalism requires accurate conceptual levels and fact boundaries, and practical reproducibility requires that materials, versions, commands, parameter functions and results can be verified. Successful operation cannot replace the first four items, and smooth writing cannot replace correctness. If you still need to know "what the user just asked" to understand why a certain paragraph appears, or if the text repeatedly restarts scenes, repeats summaries and disclaimers, the integration has not been completed. Go back to the actual paragraph and rewrite it, without adding transition words or keeping a more detailed table in place of coherence.

## 8. The rhythm of the textbook makes readers willing to continue reading.

When users use textbooks such as "C Primer Plus" and "C++ Primer Plus" to describe their goals, they transform them into executable requirements for original writing: coherent narratives with content, gradually increasing examples, hands-on observations, a moderate sense of relaxation, and exercises that prompt readers to actively reason. Don’t claim to have a textbook style just by adding a few friendly words to your technical outline.

Decide first whether this article will be a continuous textbook chapter, an independent introductory article, or a reference page. Consecutive chapters clarify the prerequisite knowledge, review one or two understandings related to this chapter, and then introduce the remaining questions; there is no need to re-teach each chapter because the file can be opened separately. Independent introductory articles must explain the premises within the scope of their commitment. The reference page should be easy to find without forcing a story for each option. The knowledge base can provide continuous routes and query entrances at the same time, reusing the same authoritative text.

The blueprint records what readers can interpret at the beginning of each stage, how they might predict it, and what new judgmental skills are added at the end. The nouns in the catalog are only indexes and cannot replace this route of cognitive change. Choose a through-truth task that is small enough and worth completing, and let the new concept solve the problem it actually exposes; the task can be a host tool, a device driver, or concurrent access, and it does not need to be artificially packaged into a character story.

Let the reader first understand a normal path and obtain a visible result. Then add a constraint or change a condition so that the old understanding is no longer sufficient, and then explain the mechanism that needs to be supplemented. Do not change languages, directory layouts, tool versions, and business goals in the same round so that readers cannot determine what caused the change in results. Nor do you need to explain all the principles before you start: you can observe first, then establish explanations, and then make predictable comparisons.

Adjacent paragraphs should be connected by reason: what questions are left by the previous results, and why the following operations can answer them. There is no need to write "therefore" and "next" in each paragraph, and you cannot mechanically repeat "what is the question - what is the answer" in each section. Let the code, output, narrative, diagrams and short exercises alternate as needed; the tables organize the differences that have been understood, and the main text is responsible for the first explanation. Each subheading should help the reader orient to the real learning task, rather than chopping up an otherwise coherent paragraph.

Allow not to know details that are not currently needed, and indicate when you will come back to explain. Deep implementation, complete parameters, and rare platform differences can be placed in subsequent sections or reference pages; what cannot be postponed are the conditions required to perform the current experiment, the prerequisites for correctness, and the boundaries that affect the results. The completeness of the explanation is ensured by the complete set of teaching materials and clear dependencies. It does not require full expansion of each paragraph, nor does it rely on subsequent links to cover up current logical gaps.

### 8.1. The text between the titles must be connected.

The reader moves past the title with the object, conditions, and open questions of the previous paragraph in mind. Before writing a section, first determine whether you are continuing the same process, zooming in on one step, comparing a variant, or entering a new task; then let the main text present this relationship. The check items are used for the author’s judgment and do not become a fixed opening template for each section:

- **Title and expansion:** The summary, judgment or name in the title can only indicate the reading direction. The main text should allow readers to go from established facts to the judgment. The title conclusion should not be directly regarded as the proven premise; nor is it required to copy the title again.
- **Scenarios and Conditions:** Explicit objects and states are used when continuing the same scene. When switching tasks, platforms, actors, time stages, or abstraction layers, explain why you switched, what changes the current reasoning relies on, and what existing knowledge still applies, without suddenly resetting the entire scene.
- **Word order and reference:** Give priority to letting known objects lead to new conditions, actions and results. The reference or cause and effect of "at this time, therefore, it, this way" must be found in the text; the object must be stated directly when necessary, and the subject and premise cannot be supplemented by the title alone.
- **Turns and experiments:** When moving from conclusion to operation, explain which judgment should be tested in this operation; explain the state left when moving from failure to recovery, and from recovery to new experiment. The explanation after the code block or table also goes back to the same question.

For example, just after finishing "Run the program with the full interpreter path of A" in the previous article, the next section is titled "Activating only changes the command lookup", but the text directly says "Therefore it does not change B", skipping the short command selection situation. can be written as:

> Just now, the interpreter path of A was specified every time it was run, so there is no ambiguity about who executes the program. If you wish to type only python, the terminal will need to select one of the available programs. The activation script will put A's executable directory in front of PATH so that this short command finds A first. When the path to B is explicitly written out, the selection is already done by the path and B will still be run.

The examples draw on the relationship from existing operations to new requirements and then to mechanisms. It is not required that each section begins with "just now". Naturally established connections are kept simple; different topics can be connected using progression, juxtaposition, contrast, or task-specific variations, without the need to invent glitches to create motivation. The reference page retains the organization method for easy inquiry and does not force the change to a continuous story.

The acceptance process is divided into two steps: first read "the last paragraph of the previous section - the title - the first paragraph of the next section" to determine why this content appears at this time; then temporarily cover the title and check whether the object, condition, reference and key cause and effect of the text are complete. Covering titles is a diagnostic approach that does not require removing titles, repeating all category labels, or making each section readable independently of the previous text. When a breakpoint is discovered, the actual explanation, word order, or content position should be modified; simply adding "next introduction", repeating the title, or increasing the word count of the paragraph does not count as a repair. Automatic scripts cannot reliably determine this semantic relationship, and must record specific repair boundaries and the limitations of their own independent reading review.

### 8.2. Use the same road map to help readers orient themselves

The overall task route is given at the beginning of the multi-step experiment, and the nodes use operable actions and stable numbers. As you begin each step, recreate the same compact roadmap, marking the current node with a high-contrast color and writing the current number. Don't rely solely on color. The node name, number and connection remain consistent, and returning to the original workspace or recovery after failure also indicates which step to return to.

A roadmap answers "Where am I now?" and a machine map answers "How does this step work internally?" Both have their uses. Iterative roadmaps allow no additional technical information, no need to redesign a diagram each time to pass deduplication checks, and no need to draw the entire book or each command into a long process. Use the steps of this chapter within the chapter, and the chapter entrance also indicates its position in the entire course to avoid having multiple sets of numbers competing for attention at the same time.

## 9. Experimentation and practice move knowledge forward

Experiments should occur where it is worth observing, comparing, or refuting a hypothesis. When arranging, first determine what kind of understanding it wants readers to change, and then select the minimum materials and operations; it is not a matter of first piling up a batch of scripts and then supplementing them with learning objectives. A successful run can provide first feedback, but the same experiment should generally also support meaningful changes in conditions. Mechanisms that cannot be implemented in the current environment can provide clearly marked paper deductions or log analysis, and cannot be pretended to be actual measurements.

Practice gradually reducing handrails: first take the reader through a normal example, then set aside predictions or small modifications, then let the reader locate a recoverable failure, and finally move to an adjacent task. Choose the required level according to the topic, and it is not required to have the same set of questions in each section. Difficulty should come from applying what has been said, not from omitting tables of contents, instrumental premises, key syntax, or expected evidence.

When asking a prediction question, give the reader enough clues and leave the answer where it can be found; do not answer the same sentence for him right after the question is asked, and do not use "think about it" instead of prompts and answers. The reasons necessary for the completion of the text still need to be explained; only derivations deliberately left as exercises are fit for postponement. The answer explains the judgment process, why common guesses are not valid, and what other methods are correct, rather than just giving the final output or standard code.

For example, readers already know that directly specifying the interpreter can select the environment, and then learn to activate A. At this time, asking him to predict "what will happen if B is specified directly", and then running and checking the actual interpreter is a well-founded comparison. For a later migration question, you can ask "How to obtain evidence when the editor runs and the terminal runs in different environments?" without having to copy the same command repeatedly. The former consolidates the selection mechanism, while the latter uses the mechanism for troubleshooting.

Keep a clear starting point and return path for the experiment, and distinguish between the original textbook, the reader-modified copy, and the generated product. Automation scripts are used for regression and repetition preparation, and the core operations the reader needs to learn should remain visible. An expected failure must explain the specific reasons and be able to re-observe after recovery; it cannot just stipulate that "it will pass if there are errors."

## 10. Equal, specific and measured tone

Treat readers as peers who are willing to think about it but have not yet come across this topic. Use accurate, ordinary language. Don't pressure the reader with "obviously," "very simple," or "a novice can do it," or repeatedly praise him for completing an order. Do not create fictional stories about teaching experiences, student feedback, or the author’s personal experiences to create a human flavor. The discovery process of the technical content itself should bear interest.

Humor can come from the contrast of just observing, relaxing briefly and then immediately returning to the explanation. There is no requirement for jokes in every chapter; no personification is allowed to replace real mechanisms, and easy pitfalls are not written as readers being stupid. Use less empty phrases such as "Isn't it amazing?" and "Happy exploration" and don't write all chapters into dramatic malfunction stories.

The following original clip only demonstrates the narrative scale; two environments and activation operations have been created previously, so the entire creation process will not be repeated in this paragraph:

> After activating A, its name already appears in the terminal prompt. Now let’s take a guess: if we explicitly run B’s interpreter, who will execute the program?
>
> Run the previous watcher, but this time change the interpreter path to B. Look at the actual interpreter location in the output first, then at the prompt.
>
> The prompt still says A, but it's B doing the work - don't rush to reload this time.
>
> The activation script makes it easier for the short command python to find A; the command just now has already specified the location of B, and there is no need to select it through the search path. The prompt describes the activation status of the terminal, and the output record is the program that was actually started this time.
>
> Next, just change the interpreter path to the short command python, and leave the rest unchanged. Based on the difference just now, who should we find this time?

The complete textbook must provide real commands, observation procedures, and practice solutions adjacent to the segment; the demonstration segment cannot be reproduced as a stand-alone experiment lacking operating materials. Nor is it a fixed beginning: it draws on clued predictions, concrete observations, a healthy dose of humor, and on-the-spot explanations.

## 11. Use sample chapters to calibrate actual writing methods

When adding a new chapter to the textbook or repairing the overall writing method, complete a representative sample chapter before expanding the entire set of text. Choose chapters that reflect concepts, operations, explanations, and exercises, and don't just write a preface that makes it easy to appear fluent. The sample chapter contains actually usable materials and solutions, verify the command or deduction first, and then check the continuous reading. If there is a sample chapter approved by the user, priority will be given to reusing the explanatory depth and rhythm without mechanically duplicating the number of titles or word count.

Point out specific and effective writing methods from the sample chapters: where new requirements are introduced, where readers are asked to predict, which paragraph pushes the output back to the principle, and what knowledge is required for practice. Subsequent chapters inherit these teaching judgments and adjust their structure according to the content. Simply saying "keep it Primer style" or requiring the same template for every chapter is not enough to convey standards.

During calibration, the technology is judged to be correct, the experiment can be run, the readers understand and the reading experience is judged respectively. Check that there is reasonable progression between paragraphs, that explanations are restarted repeatedly, that readers can discern current emphasis, that humor interrupts reasoning, and that exercises have fair clues. The modification of the length is based on the reading task: the reasons that should be supplemented are supplemented, and the details that should be moved are moved later; the number of words or the complete rules are not used as proof of quality.

If there are no real users to try it out, it will be marked as self-check by the author. When there is a complete task authorization, proceed after self-examination and verification, and do not turn the sample chapter into a process that must wait for confirmation; users provide feedback and make subsequent corrections accordingly. The final delivery is still completed within the scope of the user's request, and subsequent outlining or unfinished content cannot be covered up with a good-looking sample chapter.
